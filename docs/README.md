## Brief Overview of OctoAcme Project Management Processes

OctoAcme uses a structured, lifecycle-based project management approach to move work from idea to release in a repeatable and transparent way. Each project begins with initiation, where the team validates the business need, identifies stakeholders, and creates a lightweight one-pager with goals, success metrics, and early risks. Once the initiative is approved, the team moves into planning, breaking work into a backlog, estimating scope, aligning milestones, and documenting dependencies and quality expectations.

During execution, the team uses regular check-ins, daily standups, and milestone reviews to track progress, unblock work, and keep delivery moving. This stage emphasizes iterative delivery, clear ownership, and consistent reporting through project boards and status updates. The operating model also includes defined escalation paths for blockers and dependency issues so the team can respond quickly when risk or schedule pressure increases.

Communication and risk management are treated as core practices rather than optional overhead. OctoAcme expects regular stakeholder updates, a shared source of truth for project status, and a simple risk register that tracks impact, likelihood, ownership, and mitigation. These practices support alignment across product, engineering, and leadership while helping the team identify and act on problems before they become major issues.

Quality and release readiness are built into the workflow from the beginning. Teams are expected to define acceptance criteria, run tests in CI, validate critical flows with smoke tests, and confirm that work is complete before deployment. When a feature is ready, the team follows a release checklist that includes staging validation, production rollout, post-deploy verification, and communication to stakeholders. After each sprint or milestone, the team concludes with a retrospective to capture learning, assign action items, and improve the process over time.

## Project Management Lifecycle

OctoAcme projects follow a five-phase lifecycle:

1. Initiation — Define the problem, stakeholders, and high-level timeline
2. Planning — Create scope, milestones, dependencies, and backlog priorities
3. Execution — Build, test, review, and iterate toward milestones
4. Release — Deploy with safety checks, observability, and communication
5. Close & Retrospective — Capture learning and improve the next cycle

## Core Principles

- Customer-first — Prioritize customer value and usability
- Iterative delivery — Deliver small, testable increments
- Clear ownership — Each project has named roles for delivery and product leadership
- Data-informed decisions — Use metrics and evidence to guide iteration
- Psychological safety — Encourage feedback, learning, and candid communication

## Quick Reference: Key Roles and Terms

### Key Roles

- Project Manager (PM) — Coordinates planning, schedules, communication, and risk tracking
- Product Manager (PdM) — Defines outcomes, prioritizes backlog, and measures success
- Developers — Build, test, and deliver features and fixes
- QA / Testing — Validate quality and acceptance criteria
- Stakeholders — Provide input, approvals, and business context

### Key Terms

- Project One-pager — A concise summary of the problem, goal, stakeholders, timeline, and risks
- Backlog — Prioritized list of work items with acceptance criteria and ownership
- Definition of Done (DoD) — Shared bar for when work is complete and ready to ship
- Risk Register — A living record of risks, impact, owner, and mitigation plans
- Retrospective — A structured review of what worked, what did not, and what to improve
